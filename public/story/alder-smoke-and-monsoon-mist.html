HARVEST MONOGRAPH · MON DISTRICT · 1,460m ASL

Alder Smoke & Monsoon Mist: Curing Raja Mircha in Mon District

Why fourteen days on slatted bamboo racks above smouldering Himalayan alder hearths turns fierce heat into layered culinary depth.

HARVEST CHRONOLOGY
  1. Dawn Mist: Cool valley fog shields the crop from harsh early sun.
  2. Selective Harvest: Hand-gathered at peak volatile oil and sap density.
  3. Bamboo Curing: Slow ambient withering or hearth-loft preservation on woven bamboo.
  4. Cardiff Arrival: Small-batch inspection and sealing in UV-opaque vessels.

Cultivated in rotational jhum hill plots alongside nitrogen-fixing Himalayan alder trees in Mon District, then cured for fourteen days on bamboo racks above domestic alder-wood hearths. Across Mon District, Nagaland, the transition from monsoon humidity to crisp post-monsoon nights slows plant metabolism—concentrating aromatics before the harvest window opens.

Tasting profile in the cup or kitchen: Smouldering alder wood, dried sour cherry, black cardamom, slow-building volcanic warmth. Recommended preparation: Steep a 1cm ribbon of pod in simmering broths, dals, or chilli oils; remove before serving for gentle infusion, or pound whole for intense heat.

“When the autumn fog settles over the valley, growth slows to a whisper—concentrating essential oils and mineral character inside every harvest.”
