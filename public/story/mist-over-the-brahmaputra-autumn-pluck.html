HARVEST MONOGRAPH · DIBRUGARH · 27°28′N 94°54′E

Mist Over the Brahmaputra: The Autumn Tippy Pluck

How cool November nights slow leaf growth in Upper Assam, concentrating malted honey and muscatel aromatics in the golden terminal bud.

HARVEST CHRONOLOGY
  1. Dawn Mist: Cool valley fog shields the crop from harsh early sun.
  2. Selective Harvest: Hand-gathered at peak volatile oil and sap density.
  3. Bamboo Curing: Slow ambient withering or hearth-loft preservation on woven bamboo.
  4. Cardiff Arrival: Small-batch inspection and sealing in UV-opaque vessels.

Hand-plucked two leaves and a downy bud from smallholder gardens along the southern bank of the Brahmaputra in Dibrugarh. Withered on woven bamboo trays and gently rolled to preserve unbroken golden tips. Across Dibrugarh District, Upper Assam, the transition from monsoon humidity to crisp post-monsoon nights slows plant metabolism—concentrating aromatics before the harvest window opens.

Tasting profile in the cup or kitchen: Toasted malt, dark heather honey, stewed orchard plum, clean mineral finish. Recommended preparation: 3.5g leaf per 250ml spring water at 92°C. Infuse for 3 minutes 30 seconds; yields three distinct infusions.

“When the autumn fog settles over the valley, growth slows to a whisper—concentrating essential oils and mineral character inside every harvest.”
